Tonale Privacy Policy

Effective date: 2026-09-29 · Web copy: https://xintechllc.com/Tonale/privacy.html

Scope and Controller

This policy explains how Tonale handles information on your Mac, and when information goes to Xintech LLC or to another company. Xintech LLC is the controller of the information it receives.

Tonale is a music and playlist app for macOS 14 and later. It runs in Apple's App Sandbox, so it can open only the files and folders you give it.

You can use Tonale without an account. Your own files, your playlists, Apple Music, and YouTube videos all work while you are signed out. An account is needed only to keep your library the same on more than one Mac.

Tonale has no advertising, no analytics SDK, and no crash-reporting SDK, and it sends no background usage data to Xintech LLC. Xintech LLC does not sell personal information and does not use it for cross-context behavioral advertising.

What Tonale Keeps on Your Mac

Tonale keeps these things in its own folder on your Mac, ~/Library/Containers/com.xintechllc.tonale/. They stay on your Mac unless a section below says otherwise:

  • your playlists and saved filters, their names, and the order of their entries;
  • a reference to each item you add: for a file, its name, size, and modification date, a fingerprint of its contents, and a security-scoped bookmark (a token from macOS that lets Tonale open the file again); for Apple Music, Apple's identifiers; for YouTube, the video identifier. The fingerprint is a short one-way code made from parts of the file's sound or picture data. Tonale uses it to recognize a file that was moved or renamed, and the file cannot be recovered from it;
  • the title, artist, album, and duration shown for each item;
  • public YouTube details: title, channel, duration, thumbnail address, availability, whether the video can be embedded, and whether it is made for kids. Tonale clears these details after 30 days and gets them again when it needs them;
  • your settings, such as playback options and window sizes;
  • a copy of the library database made once a day, before the first change of the day. Tonale keeps the newest three copies. If you restore a copy, Tonale also keeps the library it replaced;
  • up to ten email addresses you signed in with on this Mac, so the sign-in form can offer them again;
  • if you choose to sign in to YouTube, Tonale's WebKit website data, which holds your YouTube session, until you sign out (see Signing in to YouTube);
  • if you connect your YouTube account, Google's tokens for that connection and your channel's name and identifiers, in the macOS Keychain on this Mac, until you disconnect (see Connecting your YouTube account);
  • if you sign in, your session, in the macOS Keychain on this Mac. It is shared only with Xin apps on this Mac that share sign-in, such as XHelpers Pro, and does not sync to iCloud.

If you sign in, Tonale keeps a separate library for your account next to the library you use while signed out.

This data stays until you delete it in Tonale or delete Tonale's folder above. Moving Tonale to the Trash does not delete it.

Tonale writes status messages to Apple's unified log on your Mac. They are meant to leave out your titles, file paths, tokens, and email address. Tonale has no log file of its own and does not send logs to Xintech LLC. macOS controls how long the unified log keeps messages.

Your Files

  • Tonale opens only the files and folders that you choose in a macOS open panel or drag into the app. It does not look through your disk.
  • Tonale does not change, move, or delete your media files.
  • If a file moves or macOS no longer lets Tonale open it, Tonale shows the item as "Unavailable". You can locate it, or let Tonale look for it in a folder you pick with Find Missing Files…. Tonale does not search your disk on its own.
  • Tonale never uploads the contents of your files and never sends their paths. If you use sync, a file is described only by its name, size, modification date, and fingerprint, and the title, artist, album, and duration shown in the app.

Apple Music

  • The first time you use Apple Music in Tonale, macOS asks for permission to use your media library. You can say no; the rest of Tonale still works. You can change this later in System Settings › Privacy & Security › Media & Apple Music.
  • Playing Apple Music content needs your own Apple Music subscription. Xintech LLC does not sell or bill for Apple Music.
  • Tonale uses Apple's MusicKit. Your requests go to Apple under Apple's Privacy Policy. Xintech LLC does not receive your Apple Music account, your listening history, or your subscription status.
  • Tonale can also read unprotected music files in your Music library, with the read-only permission macOS gives it for music files.

YouTube

Tonale plays YouTube videos in YouTube's official embedded player, in a visible window. You can also watch a video on YouTube's own page in a Tonale window (see Watch on YouTube).

  • In the player inside Tonale, the video pauses when the player is hidden, minimized, mostly covered, or moved off the screen, and when the display sleeps. That player does not play YouTube audio in the background.
  • Tonale does not download videos, save their audio, or block ads.
  • Playing a video connects your Mac directly to YouTube and Google. The Google Privacy Policy and the YouTube Terms of Service apply to that connection. Unless you choose to sign in to YouTube (see below), Tonale does not keep YouTube's cookies or website data after you quit.
  • Thumbnails load directly from the addresses YouTube provides.
  • Tonale uses YouTube API Services: for the public YouTube details below and, if you connect your YouTube account, to import your playlists.

Watch on YouTube

You can choose Watch on YouTube in the Playback menu, or on the player when a video cannot play there. Tonale then shows YouTube's own watch page for that video in a Tonale window.

  • The window shows youtube.com as a browser does, with YouTube's own cookies, ads, and controls. Tonale adds no script to the page. It observes the page's address and uses WebKit's public media-playback state, such as playing or paused, to identify the video and follow playback started in Tonale.
  • Pages other than YouTube and Google's sign-in open in your default browser.
  • If you chose to sign in to YouTube, the window uses your saved sign-in data (see Signing in to YouTube). If you did not, it uses temporary website data, which is deleted when the window closes.

Signing in to YouTube (optional)

You can sign in to YouTube in Settings › YouTube › Sign in to YouTube…, so that your YouTube sign-in applies to videos you watch in Tonale. This is optional and off until you choose it. Before your first sign-in, Tonale shows a consent sheet that explains what changes. Signing in does not import your playlists; for that, see Connecting your YouTube account.

  • You sign in on Google's own pages, in a Tonale window. Tonale receives no password or other credentials, types nothing into those pages, and reads nothing from them. To show whether you are signed in, Tonale reads only the names of cookies, never their values.
  • From the time you choose to sign in until you sign out, Tonale keeps its WebKit website data (WebKit is the web engine in macOS), which holds your YouTube session, in its folder on your Mac. Nothing about the session goes to Xintech LLC.
  • On macOS 27 or later, while you are signed in, Tonale's player loads from youtube.com instead of the privacy-enhanced youtube-nocookie.com, so YouTube and Google recognize you in the player and on YouTube's watch page.
  • On earlier versions of macOS, the player inside Tonale stays signed out, on youtube-nocookie.com. Your sign-in is used only on YouTube's watch page, in the window that Watch on YouTube opens.
  • Where YouTube and Google recognize you, your history, recommendations, and ads follow your Google settings, under Google's policies.
  • Before you choose to sign in, and again after you sign out, the player loads from youtube-nocookie.com with new, temporary website data each time a player opens.
  • To sign out, choose Settings › YouTube › Sign Out…. Signing out deletes all of Tonale's WebKit website data and closes the watch and sign-in windows.
  • Tonale includes the NSCrossWebsiteTrackingUsageDescription entry so that your sign-in can reach the player. On macOS 27 and later, this entry turns off WebKit's cross-site tracking prevention inside Tonale, whether or not you sign in. On earlier versions of macOS, it has no effect. Apart from the sign-in and watch windows, YouTube playback is the only web content in Tonale.
  • Tonale adds no advertising, analytics, or tracking of its own.

Public YouTube details

To show titles, durations, and the contents of public playlists, Tonale asks a service run by Xintech LLC at dataol.net. That service gets the details from Google with its own key. Tonale asks for your permission before it uses this service the first time.

  • The request contains only the video or playlist identifiers you asked for, and a page token for long playlists. It carries no account, token, or cookie; the service refuses requests that have them.
  • The application service saves no individual request log, requested identifiers, copy of the answer, or IP address. To limit request rates, it uses a scrambled form of your IP address in memory for one-minute rate limits.
  • Microsoft Azure hosts the service and may process connection information under its own terms.

Connecting your YouTube account (optional)

You can connect your YouTube account to import your own playlists, including private and unlisted ones, and your Liked Videos. This is off until you choose Connect YouTube… in YouTube Account (Settings › YouTube), and Tonale explains what it reads before your first connection. Connecting is separate from signing in to YouTube above: it does not sign in the player, and signing in does not connect your account. This feature uses YouTube API Services. The YouTube Terms of Service and the Google Privacy Policy apply.

  • Google's consent page opens in your default browser, not in Tonale, and names the app "Tonale". Tonale asks only for read-only access to your YouTube account (youtube.readonly). It never sees your Google password.
  • Google sends your browser back to Tonale at an address on your own Mac (127.0.0.1). Tonale opens that address only while you connect, for at most 10 minutes, and other devices cannot reach it. Your browser then shows a page on xintechllc.com that asks you to return to Tonale, or a page from Tonale itself if that page cannot be reached. Nothing from Google goes to that page. GitHub hosts it and processes the request under its own terms.
  • With that access, Tonale reads your channel's name and identifier; the playlists your channel owns, with their titles, privacy settings, and item counts; your Liked Videos list; the videos in a list you import; and those videos' details, such as title, channel, duration, and thumbnail. It never creates, changes, or deletes anything on YouTube.
  • These requests go directly from your Mac to Google, not through the service at dataol.net described above.
  • Tonale keeps Google's tokens for this connection, with your channel's name and identifiers, in the macOS Keychain on this Mac. Only Tonale can read that item, only while your Mac is unlocked, and it does not sync to iCloud. Tonale gets your channel's name again once it is 23 days old, and deletes it if that has not worked within 30 days.
  • The playlists you import are saved in your library on this Mac, in its YouTube group (Show Under in the playlist's menu). A one-way code made from your channel identifier marks them as coming from your account and never leaves your Mac. Tonale updates an imported playlist in the background once its last update is 23 days old, and when you choose Update Playlist from YouTube…. If a playlist goes 30 days without a successful update, Tonale removes what it imported for it. Titles and other details from YouTube are cleared after 30 days unless Tonale gets them again.
  • Imported playlists do not sync. If you are signed in to a Xin Account and add an imported video to one of your own playlists, sync sends that video's identifier and whether it came from a connected YouTube account. If you move an imported playlist under Playlists (Show Under in its menu), sync sends its name, which starts as its YouTube title, and its YouTube playlist identifier, and can send its videos' identifiers in the same way. Sync never sends your channel, your tokens, or video titles and other video details. On your other Macs, these items arrive as ordinary YouTube links.
  • Tonale uses this information only to show your account's playlists and to import, play, and update the ones you choose. Xintech LLC does not sell it or use it for advertising. Tonale's use and transfer to any other app of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.
  • To disconnect, choose Disconnect… in YouTube Account. Tonale deletes the Keychain item; removes the imported playlists and their videos from this Mac, including their entries in your other playlists; deletes its daily library copies, which could still hold them; and asks Google to revoke its access. If Google cannot confirm, Tonale tells you, and you can remove access on Google's page below. Disconnecting also removes what sync sent about them from your synced library. Delete YouTube Data… removes the connection in the same way, together with Tonale's other YouTube data.
  • You can also remove Tonale's access on Google's security settings page, https://security.google.com/settings/security/permissions. Tonale notices the next time it uses your account, for example when it updates imported playlists in the background, and then removes the account data as Disconnect does.

Playing YouTube in Chrome (edition from this website)

The Tonale you download from this website can also play YouTube items from its queue in a small Google Chrome window, through a Chrome extension. This is off until you set it up:

  • When you choose to set it up, you pick Chrome's native messaging folder, and Tonale writes one file there, com.xintechllc.tonale.browser.json, that lets Chrome start Tonale's helper. While Tonale can reach that folder, it writes the file again each time its library window opens.
  • The extension works only on the YouTube window it opens for Tonale. It reads that player's state (playing or paused, time, duration, volume, and whether an ad is showing) and sends it to Tonale on your Mac. It does not read other tabs, page text, your cookies, or your history.
  • Nothing from the extension goes to Xintech LLC. The Chrome window uses your normal Chrome profile, so YouTube and Google see your usual Chrome cookies and sign-in.
  • To stop, remove the extension in Chrome. You can also delete the file above from ~/Library/Application Support/Google/Chrome/NativeMessagingHosts/.

Optional Xin Account and Sign-In

A Xin Account is the account that Xintech LLC's apps share. In Tonale it has one purpose: to keep your library the same on your Macs. Tonale works fully without it.

  • Supabase is Xintech LLC's sign-in provider. It processes the email address or the Apple or Google identity that you sign in with, sign-in events, session data, and ordinary request information such as IP address and time. For Google sign-in, Supabase may also retain the name and profile-picture address supplied by Google with your email and identity. If you sign in with an email address, Supabase also processes your password or the codes it emails to you. Sign-in codes, account confirmations, and password-reset messages are sent through Xintech LLC's email provider.
  • With Sign in with Apple, macOS shows Apple's sign-in sheet, and Tonale asks Apple only for your email address. Xintech LLC's account service keeps Apple's refresh token, encrypted, so that it can end Tonale's Sign in with Apple access if you delete your Xin Account.
  • With Google, Tonale opens Google's sign-in page in a secure browser window that macOS provides. After you sign in, a page on xintechllc.com gives the result back to Tonale. Tonale never sees your Google or Apple password.
  • When you sign in once on a Mac, Xin apps that share sign-in, such as XHelpers Pro, can use the same sign-in on that Mac. When you sign out of Tonale, those apps are signed out on this Mac too.
  • Xintech LLC's account service runs on Microsoft Azure. It stores your account record with your email address; your Tonale membership and an identifier used only for Tonale; the identifier of your sign-in identity at Supabase; and a record of the Mac-wide sign-ins you use, with a random identifier for each Tonale installation. This Azure account record does not include your name; Supabase's sign-in record can, as described above. The account service does not use a hardware or device identifier.
  • If you use Sign in with Apple with Hide My Email, Apple gives Xintech LLC a private relay address instead of your own.
  • Signing out ends the session on this Mac. It deletes nothing on the server. Your synced library stays on this Mac, hidden, until you sign in to the same account again.

Syncing Your Library

While you are signed in, Tonale keeps your library in step with your account. It sends the things you made, and nothing else.

What syncs

  • playlists you made, their names, and the order of their entries, or, for a playlist linked to YouTube, its YouTube playlist identifier instead of its entries;
  • saved filters you made, their names, and their rules;
  • a reference to each item in them: for a file, its name, size, modification date, and fingerprint, so your other Macs can find the same file; for Apple Music, the ISRC and catalog identifier; for both, the title, artist, album, and duration shown in the app; for YouTube, only the video identifier and whether the video came from a connected YouTube account (see Connecting your YouTube account).

What never syncs

  • the contents of any file;
  • file paths, folder paths, or security-scoped bookmarks;
  • passwords, tokens, or other credentials;
  • the name of your Mac or any device or advertising identifier;
  • the lists Tonale builds for you from a source, such as your Apple Music library or your local files. Each Mac builds these from its own access;
  • playlists set to Show Under YouTube, including those imported from your YouTube account, unless you move them under Playlists;
  • your YouTube account connection: its tokens, your channel, and the one-way code that marks imported items;
  • your settings. No settings sync in this version.

While you are signed in, the library you used while signed out appears under "On This Mac". It is read-only there and does not sync. Only what you copy with "Copy to Synced Library" goes into your synced library.

Tonale syncs when it starts, shortly after you make a change, and every few minutes. Each request carries your session, the product name, the platform, and Tonale's version and build. Xintech LLC stores your synced library in Microsoft Azure Table storage. Because file paths never leave your Mac, a playlist that reaches a second Mac shows its files as "Unavailable" until you locate them there.

Importing from XHelpers Pro

If you also use XHelpers Pro, it can save its queue as a Tonale playlist. Pro writes a small import file in its own Application Support folder and asks macOS to open it, together with the media files, in Tonale.

  • The file contains a request identifier, the time, Pro's app identifier and name, the playlist name, and the list of file:// addresses in order.
  • It stays on your Mac. Nothing in it goes to Xintech LLC.
  • Pro deletes import files that are older than 24 hours when it writes a new one. Tonale remembers the identifiers of the last 200 imports so it does not import one twice.

Updates

The Tonale you download from this website uses Sparkle to update itself. Sparkle checks the update feed at https://xintechllc.com/Tonale/appcast.xml when you choose Check for Updates, and automatically if you allow it. It downloads updates you accept from GitHub. These requests include your IP address, the time, and a user agent with the Tonale and Sparkle versions. Tonale does not turn on Sparkle's optional system-profile report. GitHub hosts the feed and the downloads and processes these requests under its own terms.

Website Analytics

The Tonale web page at https://xintechllc.com/Tonale/ uses Umami Cloud, provided by Umami Software, Inc., to count visits and clicks on its download, support, privacy, and license links. Umami may process the page path and search, referrer, title, browser, operating system, device type, screen size, language, campaign parameters, approximate location derived from your IP address, and the name and target of each counted click. Umami states that it does not use analytics cookies or conventional fingerprinting and does not store IP addresses. This data is kept according to Xintech LLC's Umami Cloud plan and settings, currently up to six months. The Tonale app sends nothing to Umami. See https://umami.is/privacy.

How Tonale Protects Your Data

Tonale uses these measures to protect your information, including the Google user data it receives when you connect your YouTube account:

  • Least access. Tonale asks Google only for read-only access to YouTube (youtube.readonly). It cannot change or delete anything on YouTube, and it never sees your Google password.
  • Protected sign-in. Google's consent page opens in your default browser. Tonale uses PKCE (Proof Key for Code Exchange), so a sign-in code is useless to anyone but the Tonale that asked for it, and Google returns that code only to an address on your own Mac (127.0.0.1).
  • Encrypted connections. Tonale connects to Google, YouTube, and Xintech LLC's services only over HTTPS (TLS). macOS App Transport Security enforces this, and Tonale makes no exceptions to it. Requests that use your YouTube account go directly from your Mac to Google.
  • Tokens in the Keychain. Tonale keeps Google's tokens in the macOS data-protection Keychain on this Mac, which stores them encrypted. Only Tonale can read them, and only while your Mac is unlocked. They do not sync to iCloud, and Tonale sends them only to Google.
  • App Sandbox. Tonale runs in Apple's App Sandbox. Your library, including what you import from YouTube, stays in Tonale's own container on your Mac, which other apps cannot read without your permission. If FileVault is on, macOS also encrypts it on disk.
  • Synced library. If you sign in, Tonale syncs only the items listed under What syncs; for YouTube, only the video identifier and whether the video came from a connected YouTube account. They travel over HTTPS to Xintech LLC's service at dataol.net, which accepts a sync request only with your session and gives it only your own account's library. Microsoft Azure Table storage encrypts the stored data at rest.
  • Limited keeping. Tonale updates the YouTube data it keeps, or deletes it, within 30 days. Disconnect revokes Tonale's access at Google and deletes what Tonale imported. See Retention and Deleting Your Data.

Deleting Your Data

In Tonale, open Settings › Account & Sync and use the Account deletion menu. Each choice asks you to type DELETE and to sign in again, and each is permanent. An interrupted deletion continues when you come back to it.

  • Delete Tonale Data… deletes your synced library and Tonale membership on the server, and your account's library on this Mac, and signs you out. Your Xin Account and your other Xin apps stay.
  • Delete Xin Account… (when it is offered) also deletes your Xin Account and its data in your other Xin apps, clears your email address from the account service, deletes your sign-in identity at Supabase, and ends Tonale's Sign in with Apple access.
  • Delete Unfinished Xin Signup… is for when you signed in but did not finish creating a Xin Account.

Deleting does not delete your media files or the library you use while signed out on this Mac.

To remove your YouTube account connection and the playlists imported from it, see Connecting your YouTube account.

Children

Tonale is not directed to children, and Xintech LLC does not knowingly collect personal information from children. YouTube content in the embedded player comes from Google and is subject to Google's own protections.

Your Rights

Depending on where you live, you may have the right to ask for access to, correction of, deletion of, restriction of, or a portable copy of your personal information, to object to how it is used, and to complain to a data-protection authority. Most Tonale data never leaves your Mac, so Xintech LLC may not have data for a request about it. Your account, synced library, and request records are the exception. Tonale has no in-app export. To make a request, write to admin@xintechllc.com.
